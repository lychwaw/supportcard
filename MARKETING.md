# SupportCard — Product Overview & Pre-Launch Marketing Plan

---

## What SupportCard Does

SupportCard is a co-parenting coordination app for separated and divorced parents in South Africa. It replaces the chaos of WhatsApp threads, Excel spreadsheets, and paper receipts with one structured platform where both parents manage their shared child responsibilities.

**Core features:**

| Feature | What it solves |
|---|---|
| Expense requests | One parent logs a child expense (school, healthcare, activities). The other parent approves or rejects it. No more "you never paid me back" arguments. |
| Shared calendar | Both parents see custody days, pickups, school events, doctor appointments. Conflict detection warns before double-booking. |
| Custody Clock | Tracks actual time each parent spends with each child. Objective data for disputes or court. |
| Co-parent messaging | In-app messages with an AI tone filter that flags hostile language and suggests a calmer rewrite before sending. |
| My SCAI | AI co-parenting assistant. Say "log a school expense of R350" or "add a custody day on Friday" — it handles the admin. |
| Document Vault | Store maintenance agreements, parenting plans, school consent forms. |
| Compliance Dashboard | Audit trail of expenses, messages, and custody logs formatted for court. Monthly PDF reports. |
| Professional Portal | Give a lawyer or mediator read-only access to your records. |
| Child Goals | Track savings contributions toward education or activities — append-only ledger. |

**Subscription tiers:**
- **Preview** — Free, 1 child, 3 expense requests/month
- **Essential** — Calendar + documents, 20 expenses/month
- **Plus** — AI assistant (My SCAI), tone check, 3 children, 100 expenses/month
- **Premium** — Court-ready exports, professional portal, verified handoffs, unlimited everything

**Platform:** iOS + Android (React Native / Expo). Web at supportcard.co.za.

**Geography:** South Africa — ZAR pricing, POPIA compliant, SA legal context.

## Target Market

### Primary: The Conflicted Co-Parent

**Who they are:**
- Separated or divorced parents, 28–50 years old
- Minimum 1 child under 18 still requiring active co-parenting
- Currently coordinating via WhatsApp, email, or nothing at all
- Have experienced at least one "he said / she said" dispute over expenses or custody time
- Income: middle class and above (can afford R150–500/month for a tool that reduces conflict)

**Their pain points:**
- "I keep a spreadsheet but my ex ignores it"
- "I have no proof of what I've paid for"
- "My lawyer charges R2,500/hour to sort out things we could track ourselves"
- "I never know whose week it is"
- "Every WhatsApp message escalates into a fight"

**Where they are:**
- Facebook groups: "Divorced Parents South Africa", "Co-Parenting SA", "Single Parents SA"
- Reddit: r/SouthAfrica, r/Parenting
- Searching Google: "co-parenting app South Africa", "how to track child expenses after divorce", "custody schedule app SA"
- At family law attorneys, mediators, and social worker offices

### Secondary: The Legal Professional

**Who they are:**
- Family law attorneys and mediators
- Looking for tools to recommend to their clients
- Value the Compliance Dashboard and Professional Portal

**Their influence:** One attorney recommending SupportCard to 3 clients per month = significant CAC-free acquisition.

## The Built-In Viral Loop

SupportCard has an unusually strong referral mechanic: **the app only works when both co-parents are on it.** When one parent signs up, they must link the other parent's email in the Family tab. That co-parent gets an invite to join.

This means every paying user is incentivised to recruit their co-parent. The co-parent's join is free (Preview tier). The conversion question then becomes: can you convert the second parent to paid?

Design the onboarding so the inviting parent stays engaged while waiting for their co-parent to accept. The moment both parents are linked, the app becomes immediately useful — which is the conversion trigger.

## Pre-Launch Strategy: From Signup to Paying User

### Goal

Convert website signups into active users on launch day. High conversion = people who:
1. Understand the problem deeply
2. Have been "warmed" by email content before launch
3. Have a personal stake in the solution (not just curious)

### The Three Levers

#### 1. Waitlist with Founder Pricing

People who sign up before launch get a permanently discounted rate — locked in for life as long as they stay subscribed. This creates urgency and rewards early adopters.

Suggested offer:
- **Essential for R79/month** (regular R129)
- **Plus for R149/month** (regular R249)
- **Premium for R279/month** (regular R499)

Message: *"Founding member pricing. Locked forever. Ends at launch."*

Show a counter on the landing page: "X founding member spots remaining" (cap at 500 or 1,000).

#### 2. The Email Sequence (8 emails over 4 weeks)

Send this drip to everyone who joins the waitlist. The goal is not to sell — it's to make them feel understood. By launch day, they should feel like you built this specifically for them.

## Acquisition Channels (Before Launch)

### Organic (Low Cost, High Trust)

| Channel | Tactic |
|---|---|
| Facebook groups | Join "Divorced Parents SA", "Co-Parenting SA", "Single Parents ZA". Post genuine, helpful content (not ads). Answer questions about custody tracking, expense disputes. Link to the waitlist in bio only. |
| Google SEO | Write 3–5 blog posts targeting: "co-parenting app South Africa", "how to track child maintenance payments SA", "custody schedule template South Africa". These take 3–6 months to rank but convert high-intent visitors. |
| TikTok / Instagram Reels | 60-second videos: "How we handle child expenses after divorce without fighting". Show the app. Speak to the emotion, not the features. |
| Reddit | Engage in r/SouthAfrica, r/Parenting when custody/divorce threads come up. Don't pitch. Be helpful. |

### Partnerships (Leverage Others' Trust)

| Partner | How |
|---|---|
| Family law attorneys | Offer a Professional Portal account for free. Once they can see their clients' records in-app, they'll recommend the app to every new client. |
| Divorce mediators | Same approach. Mediators often see couples before attorneys — earlier in the process and more motivated to find structure. |
| Social workers | Child welfare social workers handle high-conflict families. Give them a referral code. |
| Parenting blogs / podcasts | "Raising Kids After Divorce" type content. Sponsored content or product review. |

### Paid (Once You Have Proof of Conversion)

Don't run paid ads until you know your email-to-paid conversion rate from organic. Once you know a waitlist signup converts at X%, you know your maximum cost-per-signup.

When ready:
- **Facebook / Instagram** — target: South Africa, age 28–50, relationship status divorced/separated, children at home. Budget: R150–300/day. Conversion-optimised to the waitlist landing page.
- **Google Search** — target: "co-parenting app", "custody tracking app", "child maintenance tracker SA". Capture high-intent buyers.

## Landing Page Priorities

The landing page exists to do one thing: convert a visitor into a waitlist email.

**What must be above the fold:**
1. One sentence that names the pain: *"All your co-parenting admin. In one place. No more WhatsApp fights."*
2. Social proof signals: "X parents already on the waitlist"
3. One CTA: email input + "Join the waitlist"

**What must be on the rest of the page:**
- Short feature explanations (3–4 screenshots or mockups)
- Pricing teaser with founding member discount
- Trust signals: POPIA compliant, South African-built, your data stays private
- FAQ: "Do both parents need to pay?", "What happens to my data if I cancel?", "Does my ex see everything?"

**FAQ to add — this one is critical:**
> *"Does my co-parent have to use the app?"*
> Yes — and we make it easy. When you sign up, you add your co-parent's email. They get a free account. They don't need to pay for the basic features. The moment you're both connected, everything in the app works.
